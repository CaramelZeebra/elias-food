## Ingredients
- 200 g pancetta, diced
- 2 whole onion, chopped
- 4 whole carrots, diced
- 4 sticks celery, diced
- 650 g minced meat (beef, or a beef/pork mix)
- 2 tbsp strong coffee, for deglazing
- 150 ml milk
- 1 tube tomato purée
- 500 ml beef stock
- 4 bay leaves
- 1 parmesan rind (optional — or grated parmesan at the end/table instead)
- spices, to taste

## Instructions
1. Render the pancetta in a pot over medium-low heat until the fat has rendered out (don't let it go too crisp).
2. Add onions to the fat and cook until translucent.
3. Add carrots and celery, continue cooking the soffritto until softened.
4. Brown the mince separately in a different pan, in small batches, over medium-high heat — dry the meat first, don't crowd the pan, let it sit undisturbed to develop a crust.
5. Once all batches are browned, combine the meat with the vegetables in the pot.
6. Deglaze the empty meat pan with the coffee, scraping up the fond, then pour it into the pot with everything else.
7. Add the milk to the meat and vegetables, and let it simmer down for 5-10 minutes before adding tomato — this tenderizes the meat before the acid hits it.
8. Add tomato purée and let it fry/toast for a couple of minutes to lose its raw acidity and deepen in color.
9. Add beef stock, bay leaves, parmesan (rind if using — pull out before serving; grated parmesan goes in at the end or table instead), and your spices.
10. Bring to a gentle simmer (small occasional bubbles, not a boil).
11. Simmer partially covered for the bulk of the cook, removing the lid for the last 20-30 minutes if it needs to reduce further.
12. Total time 2-4 hours: stir every 20-30 minutes and scrape the bottom to prevent sticking. Add a splash of stock/water if it's reducing too fast.
13. Check doneness: the sauce should be thick enough to hold a clear trail when a spoon is dragged across the pot's bottom.
14. Taste and adjust seasoning at the very end, since balance shifts over the long simmer.

## Notes
Since milk is in the sauce, fridge life drops to 3-4 days, not the usual week-to-week-and-a-half. Freeze portions if you want to keep it longer.
